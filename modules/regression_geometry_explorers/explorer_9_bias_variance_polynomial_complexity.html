Quick reference guide

Purpose. Use this explorer to study the bias–variance tradeoff by fitting polynomial models of different complexity to noisy samples from a fixed nonlinear function.

Controls / options

ItemMeaningHow to read it
polynomial degreeModel complexity.Low degree tends to underfit; high degree can become unstable.
noise σIrreducible observation noise.Raises the noise floor of achievable prediction error.
sample sizeTraining observations.More data usually reduces variance.
evaluation x₀Location where bias and variance are estimated over repeated samples.Edge regions often exhibit more variance.
resampleCreates a fresh displayed training sample.The Monte Carlo bias/variance calculation still averages over many samples.

Metrics

ItemMeaningHow to read it
Train MSEAverage squared residual on the displayed training set.Usually falls as degree rises.
Test-grid MSESquared error against the noiseless true function on a dense grid.Shows generalization of the mean function.
Bias² at x₀Squared difference between average fitted prediction and true f(x₀).Captures systematic model error.
Variance at x₀Variance of fitted predictions across repeated training samples.Captures sensitivity to sample fluctuations.
Noise σ²Irreducible variance.Cannot be removed by model choice.

Core mathematical identity

E[(Y−f̂(x))²] = Bias[f̂(x)]² + Var[f̂(x)] + σ² under the standard regression decomposition.

The solver retains the source’s 10⁻⁷ penalty on every coefficient, including the intercept. Variance uses 80 simulations and denominator 79. Reusing a seed makes the Monte Carlo estimate repeatable.
