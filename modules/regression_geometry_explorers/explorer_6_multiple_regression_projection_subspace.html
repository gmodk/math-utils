Multiple Regression as Projection onto a Subspace

With two centered predictors x1c, x2c, the fitted centered response \hat yc is the orthogonal projection of yc onto the plane span(x1c, x2c).

Matrix form: ŷ_c = X_c (X_c'X_c)^(-1) X_c' y_c, where X_c = [x₁c x₂c]. The residual satisfies X_c' e = 0.

1) Predictor space

The cloud in the (x₁, x₂) plane shows the predictor geometry. Stronger correlation means the cloud becomes more elongated, which makes the spanning plane nearly degenerate numerically.

2) Projection onto the predictor plane

In a reduced 3D coordinate system, the horizontal plane is span(x₁c, x₂c). The vector y_c splits as y_c = ŷ_c + e with e ⟂ span(x₁c, x₂c).

3) Observed versus fitted response

If the model fits well, the points lie closer to the diagonal y = ŷ. The highlighted observation shows one fitted value / observed value pair.

4) Sum of squares and algebraic summary

The geometric heart of multiple regression is the same as in simple regression, but now the target is a higher-dimensional subspace: R² = \| \hat y_c \|² / \| y_c \|² = \cos²(\theta), where \theta is the minimal angle between y_c and the predictor subspace.

Quick reference guide

Purpose. Use this explorer to generalize OLS projection from a line to the predictor subspace span(x1c,x2c).

Core mathematical identity

β̂=(XcᵀXc)⁻¹Xcᵀyc, ŷc=P_X yc, P_X=Xc(XcᵀXc)⁻¹Xcᵀ.

The determinant is a scale-dependent indicator, not a condition number. Singular designs produce an explicit error. Camera and highlight changes reuse the current calculation.
